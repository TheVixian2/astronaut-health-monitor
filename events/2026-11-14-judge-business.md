**How we read it** (our interpretation, not their words):

1. **"why use data, not only where".** Probably: for every data source, say
   **why** we use it, not just **where** it comes from. A list of NASA links is not
   enough, and each one needs "this is what it lets us decide".
   *@stefanalbu2010-byte: confirm that's what they meant.*
2. **The eyes.** They pointed at vision as the stakes. **Careful: the judge combined two
   separate effects.** Answer politely with both:
   - **Deformed eyeballs = SANS**: globe flattening and optic disc swelling, linked to
     the **headward fluid shift in microgravity** (mechanism still debated). Not
     radiation. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7005826/ ·
     https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10359255/
   - **Radiation = cataracts**: NASA's own astronaut studies found that even low
     space-radiation doses may raise cataract incidence and bring it earlier
     (Cucinotta 2001, 295 astronauts; the NASCA study found a small effect on cortical
     cataracts). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040088727 ·
     https://bioone.org/journals/radiation-research/volume-156/issue-5/0033-7587(2001)156[0460:SRACIA]2.0.CO;2/Space-Radiation-and-Cataracts-in-Astronauts/10.1667/0033-7587(2001)156[0460:SRACIA]2.0.CO;2.full
   - Line to use: *"Two different threats to an astronaut's sight: fluid shift deforms
     the eye (SANS), which is what we track, and radiation clouds the lens (cataracts).
     Both get worse the longer the mission."*
3. **The business questions** (UVP, what for, who for, why this product, how it's
   funded, being ahead of the market). The judge expects a **business / impact slide**.
   This isn't one of the five Space Apps judging criteria, but it is what *this* judge
   will look for. Draft answers are in `presentation/ideas.md` (2026-11-14, judge on
   business).
