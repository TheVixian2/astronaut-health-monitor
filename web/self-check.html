Light & sleep

Waiting for data…

  • 1 h before sleep: switch your module lights to Pre-sleep mode (about 2700 K, blue-depleted). Blue light around 460 nm is what suppresses melatonin most.
  • In that hour: keep screens dim or off.
  • Working day: General mode (about 4500 K). If a schedule shift is coming, Phase-shift mode (about 6500 K, blue-enriched) helps move your body clock.
  • Why it matters up here: the station sees about 16 sunrises a day, so its lights are your clock. A disrupted clock is associated with weaker immune defence.

Evidence on light, sleep timing and immunity is still emerging, much of it from animal studies. Suggestions, not a diagnosis.

Sources: NASA ISS Solid-State Lighting Assembly (NTRS) · NASA risk: Risk of Inadequate Sleep and Irregular Schedules
